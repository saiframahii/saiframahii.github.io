Introduction back to ToC

Occupant-centric control (OCC) adapts building systems to individual occupant needs in real time, jointly optimising comfort and energy use. Lighting is a primary application domain: it accounts for substantial commercial energy consumption and directly affects visual comfort, circadian health, and productivity. A systematic review of 41 OCC lighting studies reveals the field is fragmented at a foundational level, with inconsistent metrics, incompatible evaluation protocols, and no shared vocabulary for representing control actions or occupant responses. Findings cannot be compared across studies, strategies cannot be validated beyond individual implementations, and evidence cannot accumulate into generalisable knowledge. The field cannot advance without this foundation, and real-world deployment of adaptive building systems at scale cannot follow. OCCO provides a shared semantic model to close this gap, unifying sensor observations and occupant feedback within a common interoperable framework built on W3C and ETSI standards.
