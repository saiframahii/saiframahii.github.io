Abstract

Occupant-centric control (OCC) embeds real-time occupant information (presence, preferences, and activity) into building control logic to simultaneously optimise comfort and energy efficiency. Lighting systems are a primary application domain: they carry significant energy loads and directly shape occupant visual comfort. Despite growing research activity, deep methodological fragmentation (inconsistent metrics, incompatible data models, and absent shared vocabularies) prevents findings from being compared or synthesised. Without a cumulative evidence base, the field cannot establish which OCC strategies work or for whom, stalling scientific advancement and inhibiting real-world adoption of adaptive building systems. The Occupant-Centric Control Ontology (OCCO) addresses this gap by providing a semantic framework that integrates established W3C and ETSI standards and extends them to cover visual comfort metrics, control strategies, occupant feedback, and performance evaluation. Developed using the Linked Open Terms (LOT) methodology with competency questions derived from a systematic review of 41 OCC studies, OCCO unifies subjective occupant feedback with objective sensor data in a single, interoperable model.
